# Gameplay Modes

> *Three distinct play modes that structure how players experience the campaign*

---

## Overview

The Estellon campaign uses three gameplay modes to create varied pacing and player engagement:

**🎬 SCENE** - Narrative moments where PCs observe story unfold
**⚔️ ENCOUNTER** - Active gameplay requiring PC decisions and rolls
**🗺️ FREE-ROAM** - Open exploration where PCs direct the action

---

## 🎬 SCENE Mode

### What It Is
Scenes are cinematic moments where the DM narrates events, NPCs interact, and the story advances. PCs are present but primarily observers. Think of these as "cutscenes" that set up the next phase of gameplay.

### Characteristics
- **NPC-Focused:** Dialogue between NPCs, not with PCs
- **Narrative-Driven:** Story information revealed
- **Minimal Interaction:** PCs observe or have limited input
- **Sets Stakes:** Establishes threats, introduces characters, shows consequences

### When to Use
- Campaign opening/introduction
- Faction leader debates
- Enemy reveals (villain monologue)
- Aftermath of major events
- Transitions between acts
- Showing parallel events (what happens elsewhere while PCs act)

### DM Guidelines
- **Keep It Short:** 3-5 minutes max, don't lecture
- **Set the Stage:** Establish tone, conflict, atmosphere
- **End with Hook:** Transition smoothly to Encounter or Free-Roam
- **Visual Language:** "You see...", "You witness...", "You overhear..."
- **Allow Reactions:** PCs can interject if they have immediate reaction, but don't force it

### Player Guidelines
- **Stay Engaged:** This is story context for your decisions
- **Take Notes:** Important NPCs and plot details emerge
- **React Naturally:** You can gasp, whisper to party, show emotion
- **Don't Derail:** Wait for DM to transition before taking major action

### Examples

**Scene: The Duke's Decree (Act I Opening)**
```
DM: "The marketplace falls silent as Duke Valerius II steps onto the
raised platform, flanked by two guards in burnished armor. His voice
carries across the crowd:

'Citizens of Estellon, by ducal authority, I hereby decree a special
conscription. Ten able-bodied men from each district shall report for
service in the border wars. Failure to comply will result in imprisonment.'

The crowd erupts in shocked murmurs. You see a woman clutch her husband's
arm in fear. An old man spits on the ground in disgust."

[TRANSITION TO FREE-ROAM]
DM: "The Duke descends from the platform, guards dispersing to enforce
the decree. What do you do?"
```

**Scene: Faction Leaders Argue (Act II)**
```
DM: "Through the cracked door, you watch as Ashka slams her fist on the
table. 'Peaceful reform? Brennis will never yield power willingly!'

Elara's voice is calm but firm: 'Violence begets violence. We must show
we're better than the corruption we fight.'

Captain Brennis crosses his arms: 'Order must be maintained. Even if it
means force.'

The three lock eyes, the tension palpable."

[TRANSITION TO ENCOUNTER]
DM: "Ashka notices you in the doorway. 'Well? Which of us is right?'
All three turn to face you."
```

**Scene: Pellor's Revelation (Act III)**
```
DM: "As Gorok falls, Lord Pellor steps from the shadows, his unassuming
demeanor transformed. Magic crackles around his fingertips.

'You think you've won?' He laughs coldly. 'I orchestrated everything.
The conscription, the faction divide, even the siege. This city tears
itself apart while I position myself as its savior.'

Duke Valerius cowers in the corner: 'Pellor, please! I did everything
you asked!'

Pellor doesn't even glance at him. His eyes are fixed on you."

[TRANSITION TO ENCOUNTER]
DM: "Roll initiative."
```

## ⚔️ ENCOUNTER Mode

### What It Is
Encounters are structured moments where PCs actively participate through dialogue, skill checks, or combat. These are the "game" parts where dice hit the table and player choices directly determine outcomes.

### Characteristics
- **PC-Driven:** Players make decisions and roll dice
- **Structured:** Clear objectives, stakes, and resolution
- **Interactive:** Dialogue with NPCs, tactical combat, skill challenges
- **Consequential:** Success/failure has mechanical and story impact

### Types of Encounters

#### 1. **Combat Encounters**
- Turn-based, initiative order
- Tactical positioning matters
- HP, damage, conditions tracked
- Victory/defeat conditions clear

#### 2. **Social Encounters**
- Dialogue with meaningful choices
- Persuasion/Deception/Intimidation checks
- NPC reactions change based on approach
- Success/failure affects relationships

#### 3. **Skill Challenges**
- Series of skill checks to overcome obstacle
- Multiple approaches possible
- Time pressure or resource management
- Failure has consequences but rarely ends story

### When to Use
- Any mission objective
- Boss fights
- Negotiating with NPCs
- Infiltration sequences
- Chase scenes
- Diplomatic summits
- Puzzles and traps

### DM Guidelines
- **Clear Objectives:** Players always know what they're trying to achieve
- **Multiple Approaches:** Allow creativity (combat, stealth, social)
- **Track Everything:** HP, resources, time, NPC attitudes
- **Call for Rolls:** Be explicit: "Give me a Persuasion check"
- **Describe Results:** Narrate what happens after rolls
- **Know Exit Conditions:** When does encounter end? (enemy dead, NPC convinced, objective complete)

### Player Guidelines
- **Declare Actions:** Tell DM what you want to do
- **Roll When Asked:** DM calls for specific checks
- **Use Resources:** This is when you spend spell slots, items, abilities
- **Track Conditions:** Know your HP, status effects, resources
- **Roleplay Choices:** Don't just say "I persuade." Say what you say.

### Examples

**Encounter: Confronting the Tax Collector (Social)**
```
DM: "The tax collector looks up from his ledger. 'State your business.'"

PLAYER: "I want to convince him the tax increase is unlawful."

DM: "How do you approach? Persuasion, Intimidation, or Deception?"

PLAYER: "Persuasion. I cite the old tax laws I read about."

DM: "Give me a Persuasion check. You have advantage because you did
research."

PLAYER: *Rolls 17*

DM: "He hesitates, flipping through papers. 'I... I suppose there is
precedent. Perhaps we can revisit the rate.' He seems genuinely convinced."

[SUCCESS - Tax rate reduced]
```

**Encounter: Prison Break Combat (Combat)**
```
DM: "Warden Thurl draws his sword. 'Intruders! Sound the alarm!'
Roll initiative."

[TURN-BASED COMBAT BEGINS]

DM: "It's your turn, what do you do?"

PLAYER: "I use Silent Takedown on the Warden." [Using Breacher (Silver) title]

DM: "Automatic success. You strike from behind - he crumples unconscious
before the alarm is raised. You take his keys."

[ENCOUNTER CONTINUES UNTIL OBJECTIVE MET]
```

**Encounter: Chase the Agitator (Skill Challenge)**
```
DM: "The Red Fox leaps to the next rooftop. You need to keep up. Give me
an Athletics check to jump the gap."

PLAYER: *Rolls 14*

DM: "You land hard but keep your footing. The gap closes to 30 feet.
Now she's scattering caltrops behind her. Acrobatics check to avoid them."

PLAYER: *Rolls 8*

DM: "You step on one - take 1d4 damage and your speed is halved this round.
She's pulling ahead - now 60 feet away."

[SKILL CHALLENGE CONTINUES OVER MULTIPLE ROUNDS]
```

## 🗺️ FREE-ROAM Mode

### What It Is
Free-Roam is open exploration where players have agency to go anywhere, talk to anyone, and pursue goals in any order. The DM sets the scene and responds to player initiative rather than guiding them through structured content.

### Characteristics
- **Player-Driven:** PCs decide where to go and what to do
- **Open-Ended:** Multiple activities available simultaneously
- **Discovery-Focused:** Explore locations, meet NPCs, gather information
- **Time Passes:** Actions have duration, world evolves
- **Flexible Pacing:** Players control speed

### When to Use
- Between major story beats
- Investigation/information gathering
- Downtime in cities
- Preparation for missions
- Social networking
- Shopping and resource management
- After completing a major objective (players need to decide what's next)

### DM Guidelines
- **Establish Boundaries:** Tell players what's available ("The market, tavern, and docks are open to explore")
- **Populate the World:** Have NPCs with goals, random encounters ready
- **Track Time:** "That takes 2 hours. It's now evening."
- **Respond, Don't Lead:** Players state intent, you narrate results
- **Offer Hooks:** NPCs approach with offers, rumors, quests
- **Allow Failure:** Shops can be closed, NPCs can be unavailable
- **Be Patient:** Let players discuss and plan

### Player Guidelines
- **Take Initiative:** Don't wait for DM to tell you what to do
- **Explore:** Talk to NPCs, investigate locations
- **Gather Info:** Ask questions, follow up on rumors
- **Prepare:** Buy supplies, rest, strategize
- **Roleplay:** This is when you define your character
- **Discuss Plans:** Use this time to coordinate with party
- **Move Things Forward:** When ready, pursue a mission/objective

### Examples

**Free-Roam: Act I Marketplace Opening**
```
DM: "After the Duke's decree, the marketplace slowly returns to nervous
activity. The sun is high - it's midday. You have the afternoon free.
What would you like to do?"

PLAYER 1: "I want to talk to that woman whose husband might be conscripted."

DM: "You approach her. She's wiping tears. 'Please, my husband is all
we have. He can't go to war.'"

PLAYER 2: "While they're doing that, I'll check out the blacksmith's stall."

DM: "Sure. You head over. The smith, Borin, is hammering away at a
horseshoe. He looks up. 'Need something?'"

PLAYER 3: "I'll wander around listening for rumors."

DM: "Give me a Perception check."

[PLAYERS SPLIT UP, EXPLORE FREELY]

DM (after 30 min real time): "The sun is getting lower - it's late
afternoon now. You've learned several things..." [Summarizes discoveries]

PLAYER 1: "Okay, we should probably pursue the 'mysterious hooded figure'
rumor."

DM: "Great. Where do you go to find them?"

[TRANSITION TO ENCOUNTER when PCs decide on action]
```

**Free-Roam: Preparing for Heist**
```
DM: "You've decided to rob the tax carriage in three days. You're back
at your safehouse. What do you want to do to prepare?"

PLAYER 1: "I want to scout the route the carriage will take."

DM: "That'll take a full day. You'll need to make Stealth checks to
avoid guard patrols."

PLAYER 2: "While they're scouting, I'll try to buy rope and grappling hooks."

DM: "There's a general store. The merchant has rope but the grappling
hook will cost 5 gold."

PLAYER 3: "I'll see if I can recruit any help from the revolutionaries."

DM: "You know Ashka has contacts. Do you go to her directly or try to
find sympathizers yourself?"

[PLAYERS PREPARE IN DIFFERENT WAYS]

DM (after prep complete): "It's the morning of the heist. You've gathered
your gear and intel. The carriage leaves at noon. What's your plan?"

[TRANSITION TO ENCOUNTER when heist begins]
```

**Free-Roam: Between Missions**
```
DM: "Mission complete. You're back in Cobbleward. It's evening. What
do you do?"

PLAYER 1: "I want to celebrate at the tavern and see if anyone's talking
about our success."

PLAYER 2: "I'll go directly to Elara to report what happened."

PLAYER 3: "Can I use this time to craft potions?"

DM: "Yes, that'll be downtime crafting. Roll for tavern rumors, Elara
receives you in her study, and you spend the evening brewing."

[PLAYERS PURSUE INDIVIDUAL GOALS]

DM: "You all reconvene the next morning. There are three mission options
available..." [Presents choices]

[TRANSITION TO ENCOUNTER when players pick mission]
```

## Mode Transitions

### How to Shift Between Modes

#### Scene → Encounter
```
SCENE: Duke announces conscription (PCs observe)
↓
DM: "The Duke's guards approach your group. 'You there! You're of age.
Report for conscription.' What do you do?"
↓
ENCOUNTER: Social or combat encounter begins
```

#### Scene → Free-Roam
```
SCENE: Faction leaders argue about strategy (PCs observe)
↓
DM: "The meeting breaks up. The leaders go their separate ways. You're
left to decide your next move. What do you do?"
↓
FREE-ROAM: Players explore, investigate, prepare
```

#### Free-Roam → Encounter
```
FREE-ROAM: Players exploring marketplace, gathering info
↓
PLAYER: "I want to follow that suspicious cloaked figure."
DM: "You tail them to a dark alley. They suddenly whirl around: 'Why
are you following me?' Give me a Deception check."
↓
ENCOUNTER: Social encounter begins
```

#### Free-Roam → Scene
```
FREE-ROAM: Players resting at tavern after mission
↓
DM: "Suddenly, the tavern door bursts open. A messenger, bloodied and
panicked, stumbles in. 'The city is under attack! Duke Theron's army
is at the gates!' Patrons rush to the windows."
↓
SCENE: Act III siege begins (PCs observe initial assault)
```

#### Encounter → Free-Roam
```
ENCOUNTER: Combat ends, enemies defeated
↓
DM: "The last guard falls. You stand in the ransacked armory, weapons
scattered everywhere. You hear distant alarm bells - you have maybe
an hour before reinforcements. What do you do?"
↓
FREE-ROAM: Players loot, escape, decide next move under time pressure
```

#### Encounter → Scene
```
ENCOUNTER: Boss fight ends, Gorok defeated
↓
DM: "Gorok falls with a final roar. As his body hits the ground, you
hear slow, mocking applause from the shadows. Lord Pellor emerges..."
↓
SCENE: Pellor reveals his plan (PCs observe villain monologue)
↓
ENCOUNTER: Boss fight 2 begins
```

### Signaling Transitions

**DM Phrases:**
- **To Scene:** "You witness...", "The scene unfolds...", "You see..."
- **To Encounter:** "What do you do?", "Roll initiative.", "Give me a [skill] check."
- **To Free-Roam:** "You have time to...", "The city is open to explore.", "What's your plan?"

**Player Recognition:**
- **In Scene:** DM narrates continuously, rarely asks for input
- **In Encounter:** DM asks "What do you do?" and calls for rolls
- **In Free-Roam:** Players state intentions, DM responds to them

## Pacing Guide

### Session Structure Example (3-hour session)

**Opening (Scene) - 5 minutes**
- Recap previous session
- Set scene for today's events
- Establish stakes/tone

**Free-Roam - 30 minutes**
- Players explore, gather info, prepare
- Social roleplay, shopping, planning
- DM drops hooks for encounters

**Encounter 1 (Combat) - 45 minutes**
- Main mission objective
- Tactical combat or infiltration
- Success/failure with consequences

**Free-Roam - 20 minutes**
- Deal with aftermath
- Quick regroup and decision-making
- Choose next objective

**Encounter 2 (Social) - 30 minutes**
- Negotiate with NPC
- Persuasion/deception checks
- Gather crucial information

**Scene - 10 minutes**
- Show consequences of PCs' actions
- Reveal faction reactions
- Set up next session's hook

**Free-Roam - 40 minutes**
- Downtime activities
- Long rest, crafting, shopping
- Plan for next session

### Mode Distribution by Act

**Act I (Investigation):**
- Scene: 20% (Set up mystery, introduce factions)
- Encounter: 40% (Investigate clues, confront NPCs)
- Free-Roam: 40% (Explore city, build relationships)

**Act II (Factional Conflict):**
- Scene: 15% (Show faction escalation)
- Encounter: 60% (Missions, combat, diplomacy)
- Free-Roam: 25% (Prep missions, manage resources)

**Act III (Siege & Climax):**
- Scene: 25% (Show scale of siege, villain reveals)
- Encounter: 65% (Two fronts, boss fights)
- Free-Roam: 10% (Brief tactical decisions between battles)

## Common Pitfalls & Solutions

### Pitfall: Scenes Drag Too Long
**Solution:** Keep scenes under 5 minutes. If longer needed, break into Scene → Free-Roam → Scene

### Pitfall: Players Passive in Free-Roam
**Solution:** Drop immediate hooks. "A child tugs your sleeve: 'Please help, my father was taken by guards!'"

### Pitfall: Encounters Become Repetitive
**Solution:** Mix combat, social, and skill challenges. Never two combat encounters in a row without break.

### Pitfall: Unclear Mode Transitions
**Solution:** Explicitly signal: "Okay, we're moving from Scene to Free-Roam now. What do you want to do?"

### Pitfall: Players Try to Interrupt Scenes
**Solution:** Allow brief reactions, but say: "Hold that thought - this scene will set up what you can do next."
