# Game Mechanics: The Weight of the Crown

> *"In Estellon, every choice carries mechanical weight, and every roll shapes the city's fate."*

## Character Creation Process

### Step-by-Step Guide

1. **Choose Class & Race** (standard D&D 5e)
2. **Choose Role** (from available roles - see below)
3. **Apply Role Bonuses** (gold, skills, faction scores)
4. **Determine Social Access** (note which areas/NPCs you can approach)
5. **Develop Backstory** (why you came to Estellon for the Sunstone Festival)
6. **Set Initial Faction Scores:**
   - Base 5 with all factions
   - Add role modifiers
   - Note starting reputation level

### Example Character

**Lyra the Merchant Bard**
- Class: Bard 3
- Race: Half-Elf
- Role: Merchant
- Starting Gold: 50 (role) + standard starting gold
- Skills: +2 Persuasion (role bonus stacks with Bard proficiency)
- Faction Scores:
  - Revolutionary Zeal: 5
  - Coalition Strength: 5
  - City Control: 5
  - Independent Influence: 7 (+2 from role)
- Social Access: Trade District, Guild Halls, can petition Lord Pellor
- Special Ability: Market Network (once per session)

## Individual Faction Reputation System

### Overview
Each PC tracks **separate reputation levels** with each faction (1-20 scale). Different PCs can have wildly different standing with different factions.

### Starting Faction Scores
- PCs begin with 5 base reputation with all factions
- Role bonuses modify starting scores
- Prologue and Act I choices further adjust these scores

### Reputation Level Brackets

#### Levels 1-5: STRANGER/UNKNOWN
- Faction is wary or indifferent
- No special benefits
- Must prove trustworthiness

#### Levels 6-10: KNOWN/NOTICED
- Faction recognizes you as sympathetic
- Minor benefits (safe passage, basic information)
- Can take standard missions

#### Levels 11-15: TRUSTED/RESPECTED
- Considered reliable ally
- Moderate benefits (discounts, better missions, backup)
- Can access faction resources
- Social advantage in faction-controlled areas

#### Levels 16-20: INNER CIRCLE/CHAMPION
- Faction's most valued operatives
- Major benefits (free healing, rare equipment, elite missions)
- Can call on faction for significant aid
- Faction leader grants audience on request
- Bonus to all social interactions with faction members

### Faction Reputation Modifiers

#### Gaining Reputation:
- Complete faction missions: +1 to +3 (based on success/difficulty)
- Make choices aligned with faction philosophy: +1
- Use Dialogue Bingo phrases effectively: +1
- Complete Faction Leader Quest Chain steps: +2
- Save faction leader's life: +5

#### Losing Reputation:
- Fail faction missions: -1 to -2
- Make choices opposing faction philosophy: -1 to -2
- Betray faction trust: -3 to -5
- Work for opposing faction openly: -1
- Attack faction members: -5 to -10

### Multi-Faction Relationships

**PCs can maintain positive relationships with multiple factions:**
- Revolutionary Zeal AND Coalition Strength (idealist path)
- Coalition Strength AND City Control (pragmatic order path)
- Revolutionary Zeal AND Independent Influence (people's champion)

**Conflicting Loyalties:**
- High reputation with opposing factions creates roleplay tension
- Some missions may force difficult choices
- Final vault showdown requires ultimate choice

## Social Access Mechanics

### How Social Access Works

Social access represents **narrative gates**, not just dice bonuses. Each role has unique access to different parts of society.

#### Examples:

**Merchant Role:**
- Can walk into Guild Hall and request audience with Guild Master
- Other roles might need to bribe guards or sneak in
- Natural quest hook: "Master Gorvain, I need information about the tax ledgers"

**Noble Role:**
- Can request formal audience with Duke Valerius
- Other roles need intermediary or must sneak into palace
- Natural quest hook: "Your Grace, I petition you regarding the conscription decree"

**Refugee Role:**
- Automatically trusted by Cobbleward residents
- Other roles need to prove themselves first
- Natural quest hook: Street urchin volunteers information without being asked

### Using Social Access in Missions

**DM Guidelines:**
- Social access removes barriers, doesn't guarantee success
- Still requires roleplay and possibly skill checks
- Can open alternate solutions to missions
- Creates organic differentiation between PCs

**Example Mission: "Find Evidence Against Rykard"**
- **Scholar:** Can access guard records in archives
- **Noble:** Can question guard officers at officers' club
- **Refugee:** Can interview victims in Cobbleward
- **Merchant:** Can bribe corrupt quartermasters for supply records

All paths can work, but each role has a natural advantage in their sphere.

## Handling Split Party Factions

### Core Principle
**AVOID PvP COMBAT.** Keep conflict in-character and collaborative, not competitive.

### Recommended Approach: Multiple Mission Tracks

**How It Works:**
- Each PC pursues missions for their chosen faction
- Successful missions add to that faction's Progress Track
- If 2 PCs support Ashka and 1 supports Elara:
  - Ashka missions: +2 Revolutionary Zeal per success
  - Elara missions: +1 Coalition Strength per success

**Benefits:**
- Creates interesting party dynamics
- No forced PvP
- All PCs contribute meaningfully
- Reflects realistic political divisions

### Alternative Option: Negotiation at the Vault

**The Vault Showdown (End of Act II):**
- If party is split, they must negotiate in-character
- Set 5-10 minute timer
- PCs must reach consensus on which faction to support
- **If they fail to agree:** All three factions fail, external invasion is catastrophic, extra casualties in Act III

**This encourages:**
- Genuine roleplaying and compromise
- Character development
- Collaborative problem-solving
- Consequences for gridlock

### Alternative Option: Synthesis Path

**The Unity Ending:**
- If PCs are divided, they can forge an alliance
- Requires successful Persuasion checks with ALL faction leaders (DC 18-20)
- Instead of one faction claiming the Mandate, they share power
- Creates unique "Council of Three" outcome
- More difficult but rewarding path

### Majority Rules with Consequences

**How It Works:**
- Faction with most PC support wins the vault
- PCs who supported other factions face relationship consequences
- Example: Party helps Ashka, but one PC is loyal to Elara
  - That PC loses -5 Coalition Strength
  - But gains +2 Revolutionary Zeal for "standing by friends"
  - Creates interesting character arc

## Party Composition Recommendations

### Balanced Party (Recommended)
- 1 Face/Social character (Merchant, Noble, Pilgrim)
- 1 Combat specialist (Sellsword)
- 1 Knowledge/Investigation (Scholar)
- 1 Stealth/Street contact (Refugee, Artisan)

**Benefits:** Can handle all quest types effectively

### Faction-Focused Parties

#### Elara's Reformers:
- Noble (political access)
- Scholar (investigation)
- Pilgrim (moral authority)
- Sellsword (protection)

#### Ashka's Revolution:
- Refugee (street knowledge)
- Artisan (working class ties)
- Sellsword (muscle)
- Merchant (resources)

#### Brennis's Guard:
- Sellsword (military respect)
- Noble (legitimacy)
- Merchant (pragmatic support)
- Scholar (tactical planning)

### "Wild Card" Party
- All different factions = Maximum political tension
- Extremely challenging but rewarding roleplay
- Requires mature players who enjoy character conflict

## Dialogue Bingo Integration

### How It Works with Roles

Roles influence which Bingo phrases feel natural:

#### Lawful Good Phrases:
- Natural for: Noble, Pilgrim, Scholar
- Awkward for: Refugee, Sellsword

#### Chaotic Good Phrases:
- Natural for: Refugee, Artisan, Merchant
- Awkward for: Noble (depending on character)

#### Lawful Neutral Phrases:
- Natural for: Scholar, Sellsword, Noble
- Awkward for: Refugee, Pilgrim

**Encourage players to:**
- Adapt phrases to their role's voice
- Earn bonuses for creative reinterpretation
- Use role as guide, not restriction

### Dialogue Bingo Rewards
- Single phrase used well: Small bonus (Inspiration, +1 to a Faction Track)
- Complete a row/column: Potent Favor (free healing, key information, temporary immunity from that faction)

## Design Philosophy

This system creates **mechanical differentiation** while encouraging **narrative-driven gameplay** and **meaningful player choices** that tie directly into the campaign's political themes.

### Key Principles:
- **Roles Define Access, Not Ability:** Social gates matter more than dice bonuses
- **Reputation Has Consequences:** Individual tracking creates personal stakes
- **Split Parties Create Drama:** Political division enhances roleplay
- **Every Choice Matters:** Mechanical weight reinforces narrative importance
