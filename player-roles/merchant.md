# The Merchant

> *"In Estellon's markets, every deal is political, and every trade carries consequences."*

## Role Overview

As a Merchant, you are a master of commerce and negotiation, arriving in Estellon with the caravan for the Sunstone Festival. Your keen eye for opportunity sees profit in chaos, but also understands that stable markets require stable cities. You navigate the political turmoil through the lens of trade, supply, and economic influence.

## Background Hooks

### Why You're Here
- **Festival Trader:** Brought rare goods specifically for the Sunstone Festival's lucrative markets
- **Guild Representative:** Sent by distant merchant guilds to establish new trade routes
- **Independent Entrepreneur:** Seeking to escape oversaturated markets in other cities
- **War Profiteer:** Following conflicts and upheavals where goods command premium prices

### Starting Resources
- Trade contacts in multiple districts
- A wagon of goods (player's choice of specialty)
- Letters of credit from merchant guilds
- Knowledge of black market connections

## Starting Bonuses (Mechanical)
- +50 gold pieces
- +2 to Persuasion checks
- +2 Independent Influence faction score
- Proficiency with merchant's tools (if not already proficient)

## Social Access
- **Can freely enter the Trade District and Market Quarter** - Your merchant credentials open doors
- **Recognized by Guild Masters and Merchant Nobles** - Fellow traders acknowledge your status
- **Can request audiences with Lord Pellor (Duke's Chamberlain)** - Economic matters grant political access
- **Access to merchant networks and trading houses** - Information flows with goods

## Special Ability
**Market Network** - Once per session, can gather rumors or locate rare items through merchant contacts. Your web of trade connections spans the city and beyond.

## Faction Perspectives

### Supporting the Reformers (Elara)
- **Economic Stability:** Reformed systems create predictable markets
- **Legal Protections:** Fair laws protect honest merchants
- **Guild Influence:** Coalition government values merchant input
- **Long-term Thinking:** Sustainable reform ensures future profits

### Supporting the Revolution (Kael)
- **Market Liberation:** Breaking monopolies creates opportunities
- **People's Economy:** Empowered workers become better customers
- **Revolutionary Supplier:** Rebels need equipment and supplies
- **New Order Architect:** Shape the economy of the new system

### Supporting the Guard (Brennis)
- **Order Equals Profit:** Stable authority ensures safe trade
- **Protected Routes:** Strong guard presence secures caravans
- **Government Contracts:** Military needs create steady income
- **Status Quo Benefits:** Current system already favors established merchants

## Unique Opportunities

### Economic Leverage
- Control supply chains during the crisis
- Manipulate prices to pressure factions
- Fund operations through "donations"
- Information broker through trade networks

### Merchant-Specific Quests
- **Supply Lines:** Secure critical resources for chosen faction
- **Economic Warfare:** Undermine opposing factions through trade
- **Market Manipulation:** Create shortages or surpluses strategically
- **Trade Negotiations:** Broker deals between unlikely allies

### Special Assets
- **Warehouse Access:** Hidden caches throughout the city
- **Merchant Contacts:** Information network through traders
- **Economic Intelligence:** Understanding of city finances
- **Bribery Resources:** Wealth to influence key figures

## Roleplaying Guidance

### Core Motivations
- Profit (but define what kind - quick or sustainable?)
- Market control and trade dominance
- Economic influence over political power
- Protecting investments and trade routes

### Key Questions
- Is stability or opportunity more profitable?
- Can ethics and profit coexist in revolution?
- Who will honor contracts after the dust settles?
- What is the true cost of principles?

### Merchant Phrases for Dialogue Bingo
- *"Every crisis has a price and a profit."*
- *"A stable coin is worth more than a shaky crown."*
- *"The market will decide who truly rules."*
- *"I deal in goods, not ideologies."*
- *"Supply and demand care nothing for justice."*

## Mechanical Suggestions

### Recommended Skills (System-Agnostic)
- Persuasion and negotiation
- Insight into motivations
- Investigation of markets
- Deception for haggling

### Potential Backgrounds
- Guild Artisan
- Noble (merchant family)
- Folk Hero (generous trader)
- Criminal (smuggler)

### Starting Equipment Ideas
- Ledgers and contracts
- Sample cases of goods
- Merchant guild seal
- Hidden coin pouches
- Letters of introduction

## Campaign Integration

### Act I Opportunities
- Profit from festival chaos
- Establish black market connections
- Choose which faction to supply

### Act II Developments
- Control critical supply chains
- Broker information through trade
- Fund faction operations

### Act III Decisions
- War profiteering vs. civic duty
- Economic sabotage options
- Shape post-siege economy

## Playing a Merchant

Remember that in Estellon's political crisis, economic power is political power. Your control over goods and trade makes you invaluable to all factions, but also makes you a target. Every transaction is a choice about the city's future, and every deal you make shapes the conflict's outcome.

Whether you're a principled trader seeking fair markets, an opportunist exploiting chaos, or a visionary building a new economy, your merchant's journey through Estellon will prove that sometimes the ledger is mightier than the sword.
